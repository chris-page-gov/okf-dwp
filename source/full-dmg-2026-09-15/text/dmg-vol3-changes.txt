January 2025 update
Chapter 09
The amendment has been added to the DMG at 09236 after a DM stated that there
was nothing in the DMG about capacity to disclose, an example has also been added.

4. As well as being aware that a change has occurred, the claimant has to be capable
of disclosing it2. That is, the claimant must possess the cognitive capacities and motor
skills that are necessary in order to carry out the particular type of communication he
or she is required to make. If a person cannot make an effective disclosure, he or she
is under no legal duty to do so.

1 R(SB) 54/83; R(SB) 15/87; Hinchy v Secretary of State for Work and Pensions; R(IS)
7/05; 2 PS v Secretary of State for Work and Pensions (SPC) [2016] UKUT 0021
(AAC) (paragraph 30).

Example

Lauren has not got an appointee and is in receipt of PIP. She was admitted to hospital
after a heart attack and her hospital stay lasted 4 months. As a result, she was paid
more PIP than she was entitled to. Medical evidence shows that she has advanced
senile dementia and Lauren’s mental state was such that she could not have the
capacity to disclose the material fact that she had been admitted to hospital. The DM
deemed that there was no recoverable overpayment.
