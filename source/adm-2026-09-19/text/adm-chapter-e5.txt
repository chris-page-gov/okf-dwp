How to determine total entitlement to welfare benefits



Welfare benefit not awarded for a month
E5017 Where a welfare benefit is awarded in respect of a period that is not a month, the monthly amount

is calculated in the same way as for unearned income1. Where this calculation results in a fraction of a
penny that fraction is


1. disregarded if it is less than half a penny or


2. treated as a penny if it is half a penny or more2.


Note: See Chapter H5 for guidance on the calculation of the monthly amount of unearned income.


                                                                    1 UC Regs, reg 80(5); reg 73; 2 reg 6(1)


Example 1


Alison is entitled to WPA of £156.65 a week. The monthly amount is £678.82 (i.e. £156.65 x 52/12).


Example 2
Howard and Bernadette have 10 children. Bernadette receives CHB of £752.60 every four weeks, her
entitlement being £188.15 a week. Their eldest child Chloe leaves school in June 2026. However, CHB for
Chloe remains in payment until the terminal date. For the period spanning the terminal date, Bernadette
receives CHB of £716.80, her entitlement being two weeks of £188.15 and two weeks of £170.25.
Thereafter, Bernadette receives CHB of £681.00 every four weeks, her entitlement being £170.25 a
week. Howard and Bernadette’s assessment period ends on the 15th of every month. For the
assessment period ending 15.8.26, the monthly amount is £815.32 (i.e. £188.15 x 52/12) and for the
assessment period ending 15.9.26 and subsequent periods until any further change it is £737.75 (i.e.
£170.25 x 52/12) (see Chapter A4 for guidance on supersession where a relevant benefit, including CHB,
ceases or is reduced mid assessment period).




Amount of welfare benefits
E5018 Unless E5019 - E5022 apply, the amount of a welfare benefit used to determine total entitlement
to welfare benefits is the amount to which a


1. single person or


2. couple


is entitled during the reference period1. Where the welfare benefit is taken into account as unearned

income for the purposes of an award of UC, the amount used is the amount taken into account 2.


                                                                1 UC Regs, reg 80(1); 2 reg 80(4) & reg 66


E5019 Where


1. there is entitlement to a welfare benefit but


2. that welfare benefit is


      2.1 not paid or


      2.2 reduced


because of receipt of an overlapping benefit1 the amount of that welfare benefit used to determine total

entitlement is the amount in payment2.


                                                                     1 SS (OB) Regs; 2 UC Regs, reg 80(1)


Example


Cecilia is entitled to SDA of £103.85 a week and WP of £47.00 a week. As SDA and WP are overlapping
benefits, Cecilia receives WP in full (£47.00 a week) and SDA of £56.85 a week (i.e. £103.85 - £47.00).
The DM decides that the monthly amount of SDA used to determine Cecilia’s total entitlement to SDA as
a welfare benefit is £246.35 (i.e. £56.85 x 52/12). The monthly amount of WP is £203.67 (i.e. £47.00 x
52/12).


E5020 Application of the BC will reduce the level of entitlement to UC. Therefore, when calculating total
entitlement to all prescribed welfare benefits for the purposes of the BC, DMs should use the level of

entitlement before application of the BC. Where the welfare benefit is UC, the amount 1 used is that

before any reduction2


1. in accordance with E5031 et seq or


2. for a sanction3.


Note: See E5013 for guidance on welfare benefits and Chapters K1 - K9 for guidance on sanctions.


                                           1 UC Regs, reg 80(2); 2 reg 81; 3 reg 80(2); WR Act 12, s 26 & 27


Example


Richard and Jacqueline claim UC. They live in Greater London and have two children. Their UC
entitlement is £2,274.85. However, Jacqueline has a higher-level sanction due to ceasing paid work by
reason of misconduct. A reduction of £331.54 is imposed. As the amount of £2,274.85 is higher than the
relevant amount of £2,110.25, the DM decides that the BC applies. The DM also decides that the
reduction of £331.54 for Jacqueline’s sanction is deducted from the relevant amount of £2,110.25 so that
Richard and Jacqueline receive UC of £1,778.71.

Reduction of universal credit E5031 - E5041



E5031 Where the BC applies to an assessment period for an award of UC, the amount of the award is
reduced


1. by the excess1 or


2. unless E5033 applies, by the excess minus any amount included in the award for the childcare costs

element in relation to that assessment period 2.


Note: See Chapter F7 for guidance on the childcare costs element.


                                                                        1 UC Regs, reg 81(1)(a); 2 reg 81(1)(b)


E5032 For the purposes of E5031, the excess is the total amount of welfare benefits that the


1. single person is or


2. couple are


entitled to in the reference period, minus the relevant amount 1 (see E5006).


                                                                                         1 UC Regs, reg 81(3)


Example 1


Raj claims UC. He is single, lives in Greater London and is not responsible for a child or qualifying young
person. Raj’s UC amount is £1,632.56. This exceeds the relevant amount of £1,413.92. The DM reduces
Raj’s UC amount by £218.64, the amount of the excess.


Example 2


Kevin and Tanya claim UC. They do not live in Greater London and have four children but do not have
childcare costs. Tanya is pregnant and receives MA of £194.32 a week. Their UC amount is £1,040.68 (i.e.
£1,882.73 from a standard allowance plus four child elements, less £842.05 MA). Tanya also receives
CHB of £323.00 every four weeks, her entitlement being £80.75 a week. Kevin and Tanya’s total welfare
benefits are £2,232.65 (i.e. UC of £1,040.68, CHB of £349.92 (£80.75 x 52/12) and MA of £842.05
(£194.32 x 52/12)). This exceeds the relevant amount of £1,835.00. The DM reduces Kevin and Tanya’s
UC by £397.65, the amount of the excess.


Example 3


Ivan and Bridget claim UC. They do not live in Greater London, have four children and have joint monthly
earnings of £390.00 with childcare costs of £450.00 a month. Their UC amount is £2,901.60 (i.e.
standard allowance plus elements and 85% childcare costs, less earnings). Bridget also receives CHB of
£323.00 every four weeks, her entitlement being £80.75 a week. Ivan and Bridget’s total welfare
benefits are £3,251.52 (i.e. UC of £2,901.60 and CHB of £349.92 (£80.75 x 52/12)). This exceeds the
relevant amount of £1,835.00. The DM reduces Ivan and Bridget’s UC by £1,034.02 (i.e. the amount of
the excess (£1,416.52) minus the childcare costs element, which is 85% of their costs (£382.50)).


E5033 E5031 2. does not apply where the childcare costs element is greater than the excess 1.


Note: The childcare costs element is also subject to a cap, so the amount may not be 85% of the costs.


                                                                                     1 UC Regs, reg 81(2)


Example


Leonard and Penny claim UC. They live in Greater London, have one child and have joint monthly
earnings of £825.00 with childcare costs of £500.00 a month. Their UC amount is £2,238.38 (i.e.
standard allowance plus elements and 85% childcare costs, less earnings). Penny also receives CHB of
£108.20 every four weeks, her entitlement being £27.05 a week. Leonard and Penny’s total welfare
benefits are £2,355.60 (i.e. UC of £2,063.45 and CHB of £117.22 (£27.05 x 52/12). This exceeds the
relevant amount of £2,110.25. However, the amount of childcare costs element, which is 85% of their
costs (£425.00) exceeds the excess (£245.35). Therefore, the DM does not reduce Leonard and Penny’s
UC.

Earnings



E5051 The BC does not apply to an award of UC in relation to an assessment period where
1. the


         1.1 claimant’s earned income or


         1.2 couple’s combined earned income (if the claimant is a member of a couple)


is equal to or exceeds the amount that a person would be paid at the higher NMW rate 1 (the national
living wage) for 16 hours a week, converted to a net monthly amount by multiplying by 52 and dividing by

122 or


2. the assessment period


         2.1 falls within a grace period (see E5052) or


         2.2 is an assessment period in which a grace period begins or ends 3.


Note 1: Earned income does not include any income a person is assumed to have because of the

minimum income floor4 (see Chapter H4).


Note 2: See Chapter H3 for guidance on the calculation of earned income for employed earners and
Chapter H4 for guidance on the calculation of earned income for S/E earners.


Note 3: The calculation at 1. can be met by a combination of employed and S/E earnings.


Note 4: For the purposes of the calculation at 1., any fraction should be rounded down to the nearest

whole pound5.


                                                   1 NMW Regs, reg 4; 2 UC Regs, reg 82(1)(a); 3 reg 82(1)(b);
                                                                           4 reg 82(4), reg 62; 5 reg 6(1A)(za)


Example


Gill claims UC and has LCW (but not LCWRA). She is single, does not live in Greater London and is not
responsible for a child or qualifying young person. She is a S/E hairdresser and has earnings of £896.66
in the assessment period. Gill’s UC amount is £1,311.59 (i.e. standard allowance plus elements, less
earnings). Gill’s total welfare benefits exceed the relevant amount of £1,229.42. However, as Gill’s
earnings exceed the higher NMW rate for 16 hours a week in the assessment period (£12.71 x 16 hours x
52 weeks / 12 months = £881.22, rounded down to £881), the DM decides that the BC does not apply.




Meaning of grace period
E5052 A grace period is a period of 9 consecutive months
1. which begins on the most recent of


      1.1 a day falling within the current period of entitlement to UC which is the first day of an
      assessment period in which


            1.1.a the claimant’s earned income or


            1.1.b the couple’s combined earned income (if the claimant is a member of a couple)


      is less than the amount calculated in E50511 or


      1.2 a day falling before the current period of entitlement to UC which is the day after a day on

      which the claimant has ceased paid work2 and


2. in respect of which the condition in E5053 applies.


Note 1: For the purposes of E5052 1.2 the grace period will still apply if there is a gap between the
claimant ceasing paid work and making a claim to UC as long as the condition in E5053 applies.


Note 2: Where the conditions for a grace period to begin are met the grace period will continue
uninterrupted for the full nine months.


                                                                         1 UC Regs, reg 82(2)(a); reg 82(2)(b)


E5053 E5052 applies where, in each of the 12 months immediately preceding the day in E5052 1.1 or 1.2


1. the claimant’s earned income or


2. the couple’s combined earned income (if the claimant was a member of a couple)


was equal to or exceeded the amount calculated in E5051 1.


                                                                                         1 UC Regs, reg 82(3)


Example 1


Cliff and Jean claim UC on 1.11.25 as Cliff finishes work on 31.10.25. On that day Cliff received earnings of
£3,592.27. He had received that amount in each of the preceding 12 months. They live in Greater London,
have no children and no other income. Cliff and Jean’s UC amount is £2,329.06 (i.e. standard allowance
plus elements). This exceeds the relevant amount of £2,110.25. However, as Cliff’s earnings exceeded
the amount in E5051 1. in each of the preceding 12 months, the DM decides that Cliff and Jean are
eligible for a grace period which will run from 1.11.25 to 31.7.26 and that the BC does not apply until
1.8.26.


Example 2
Sheldon and Amy receive UC of £2,369.15 (i.e. standard allowance plus elements, less earnings). Their
AP ends on the 12th of each month. They have two children but no childcare costs. In addition to UC,
Amy receives CHB of £173.20 every four weeks, her entitlement being £43.30 a week. Sheldon also has
earnings of £1,030.57, which he had received for more than 12 months. The BC did not apply. Sheldon’s
hours reduce and on 31.3.26 he receives £823.41. The DM decides that Sheldon and Amy are eligible for
a grace period which will run from 13.3.26 to 12.12.26 and that the BC does not apply until 13.12.26.


Example 3


Tony finishes work on 31.1.26. He lives in Greater London and claims UC on 1.2.26. His UC amount is
£1,550.14 (i.e. standard allowance plus elements). This exceeds the relevant amount of £1,413.92. On the
day he finished work Tony received earnings of £1,360.25. However, he only received that amount for 6
months. Before that 6 month period Tony’s earnings were less than the amount in E5051 1.. The DM
decides that Tony is not eligible for a grace period and applies the BC.


Example 4


Matthew and Mary claim UC on 31.5.25 as Matthew finishes work on 30.5.25. On that day Matthew
received earnings of £2,645.39. He had received more than that amount in each of the preceding 12
months. They do not live in Greater London and have no children. Mary has earnings of £403.98.
Matthew and Mary’s UC amount is £1,919.87 (i.e. standard allowance plus elements, less earnings). This
exceeds the relevant amount of £1,835.00. However, although Mary’s earnings are less than the the
amount in E5051 1., the DM determines that Matthew and Mary are eligible for a grace period because of
Matthew’s previous earnings which exceeded the relevant amount. The grace period will run from
31.5.25 to 27.2.26 and the BC does not apply until 28.2.26.


Example 5


Faye finishes work on 24.1.26, having earned £2,249.95 a month for the previous 12 months, but does
not immediately make a claim for UC while she seeks new employment and can live off her savings. On
13.4.26, having not yet found new employment, Faye makes a claim for UC. The DM determines that the
grace period will run until 25.10.26 and the BC will therefore not apply until, at the earliest, the
assessment period beginning 13.11.26.


Example 6


Varada leaves her employment, at which she earned £1,565.85 each month for the previous 12 months,
on 11.2.26. Three months later, Varada successfully obtains new employment which begins on 1.6.26, but
the role has a lower salary so Varada makes a claim for UC on 17.5.26. A DM nevertheless determines
that, as Varada had ceased paid work, a grace period will run from 12.2.26 to 11.11.26 and the BC will not
apply until, at the earliest, the assessment period beginning 17.11.26.
