Background
4. From 1.1.21, individuals and their family members moving between the UK, Ireland, Gibraltar,
Switzerland, Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway, who are not covered by the


      1. EU-UK-WA or


      2. UK-EEA EFTA Separation Agreement or


      3. UK-Swiss Citizens’ Rights Agreement


  can no longer rely on protections under EU law for social security co-ordination.


5. The UK has made reciprocal arrangements with


    1. Ireland1
    2. Gibraltar2


    3. Switzerland3


    4. Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway4


      which gives effect to Social Security co-ordination between the UK and those countries from the
      dates as shown at paragraph 2 of this memo.

                                                       4 SS (Iceland) (Liechtenstein) (Norway) Order 2023


6. The Trade & Co-operation Agreement1 [TCA] social security protocol provides for social security co-
ordination between EU Member States and the UK from the end of the transition period on 31.12.20 and
is applied from 1.1.21 for those not in scope of the EU-UK-WA.

Ireland

7.The Schedule to the 2019 Order1 broadly replicates existing legislation2 ensuring that there is no
change in the social security rights of British nationals, Irish nationals and their family members moving
between the UK and Ireland as a result of the UK’s withdrawal from the EU.


                                                   1 SS (Ireland) Order 19; 2 Regulation (EC) No 883/2004



Changes

8. PIP Daily Living is classified as a long-term care benefit under the 2019 Order 1. When making a claim
for long-term care benefits from the UK, persons moving from


      1. the UK to Ireland are able to benefit from the broader legal test of whether they can show a
      ‘genuine and or sufficient link’ to the UK


      2. Ireland to the UK, the narrower ‘past-presence’ legal test is applied (see Note).


Note: Periods of insurance in Ireland will be applied to said persons’ when the PPT is applied to them. For
full guidance on the PPT see ADM Chapter C2.


                                                                                     1 SS (Ireland) Order 19


9. The social security rights of UK and Irish nationals provided for under the Common Travel Area
agreement are unchanged by the 2019 Order1.
                                                                                     1 SS (Ireland) Order 19



Gibraltar
10. For persons not in scope of the


   1. EU-UK-WA or


   2. UK-EEA EFTA Separation Agreement or


   3. UK-Swiss Citizens’ Rights Agreement or


   4. UK-Swiss Social Security Convention


      the Schedule to the 2024 Order1 replicates previous legislation2 ensuring that there is no change
      to the social security rights of individuals and their family members moving between the UK and
      Gibraltar as a result of the UK’s withdrawal from the EU.


                                             1 SS (Gibraltar) Order 2024; 2 Regulation (EC) No 883/2004


11. The 2024 Order1 came into effect on 1.6.24. To ensure continuity, claim and awards made


      1. for periods on or before 31.12.20 will be determined under the earlier agreement2, which

      provided that previous legislation3 applied or


      2. from 1.1.21 will be determined under the 2024 Order1. However, Article 65 (3) does not permit
      claims to be backdated between 1.6.24 to 1.1.21 (i.e. no awards should be backdated between
      those two dates).


                                 1 SS (Gibraltar) Order 2024; 2 Exchange of letters (Gibraltar) Order 1974;

Switzerland

12. The Schedule to the 2021 Order1 has fewer or modified social security rights and applies to all
individuals and their family members moving between the UK and Switzerland with the following
exceptions


      1. for Benefits in Kind, only Swiss, UK and EU nationals (plus stateless persons and refugees) are in
      scope for UK healthcare where the UK is competent and


      2. only Swiss, UK and EU nationals (plus stateless persons and refugees) are in scope for Swiss
      cash benefits and healthcare where Switzerland is competent.
      Note: See paragraph 14 for a further explanation of the changes to social security rights under the
      2021 Order.


                                                                              1 SS (Switzerland) Order 2021



Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway

13. The Schedule to the 2023 Order1 has fewer or modified social security rights for individuals and their
family members moving between the UK, Iceland, Norway, and Liechtenstein. See paragraph 14 for a
further explanation of those changes.



                                                        1 SS (Iceland) (Liechtenstein) (Norway) Order 2023




Changes

14. For Switzerland, Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway, social security co-ordination under the TCA 1, the

2021 Order2 and the 2023 Order3 differ from the rights that exist for persons covered by the EU-UK-WA.

The following changes apply for


      1. PIP, Daily Living is no longer exportable


      2. JSA (C), the aggregation provisions continue to apply but there is no provision to export the
      benefit


      3. ESA (C), the benefit is co-ordinated as an invalidity benefit from the outset. Aggregation
      provisions continue to apply, but there is no provision for exporting the benefit.


                1 TCA; 2 SS (Switzerland) Order 2021; 3 SS (Iceland) (Liechtenstein) (Norway) Order 2023



Interim provisions

Norway

15. From the end of the transition period on 31.12.20 to the entry into force of the 2023 Order 1 on 1.1.24,
the UK and Norway applied the 1991 Agreement between them as amended by 2020 Exchange of

Letters. These were implemented by the Social Security (Norway) Order 2020 2.


                                                        1 SS (Iceland) (Liechtenstein) (Norway) Order 2023;


                                                      2 The Social Security (Norway) Order 2020 No. 1597
16. From 1.1.21 for those who were not in scope of the Separation Agreements, only pensions were
coordinated. This means that for claimants between 1.1.21 and 31.12.23 there were fewer rights but this
was temporary pending the new agreement and only applied to those who were not covered by the
Separation Agreements.


        Note: The Separation Agreements are shown at paragraph 5 and are listed 1-4.
