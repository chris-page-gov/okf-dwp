# Terms you may meet

Use this page when a term appears in the [learning path](learning-path.md), a
question or an evidence package. These explanations describe the project and
help readers distinguish benefits; they are not eligibility rules or advice.
Official links were checked on 19 September 2026. Current official information
can differ from the project's frozen source collection.

## Benefit names

### DWP, DMG and ADM

- **DWP — Department for Work and Pensions:** the department publishing the
  guidance used here.
- **DMG — Decision makers’ guide:** published guidance for staff making benefits
  and pensions decisions. Its presence in the collection does not make every
  passage applicable to a current case. [Official collection](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/decision-makers-guide-staff-guide).
- **ADM — Advice for decision making:** a separate DWP guidance family, including
  guidance for Universal Credit and Personal Independence Payment. Check the
  applicable benefit and regime before choosing a guide.
  [Official ADM publication](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/advice-for-decision-making-staff-guide).
  The [separate ADM acquisition](adm-acquisition.md) preserves all 182 PDFs listed
  in the project's 19 September 2026 observation.

### PC, SPC, SP and SPA

- **PC — Pension Credit; SPC — State Pension Credit:** names used for Pension
  Credit in this project and the guidance. It is separate from State Pension.
  [Official Pension Credit guide](https://www.gov.uk/pension-credit).
- **SP — State Pension:** a state retirement pension. Basic and new State Pension
  are distinct regimes; the short name alone does not select the rules.
  [Official State Pension guide](https://www.gov.uk/new-state-pension).
- **SPA — State Pension age:** the age at which a person can start receiving State
  Pension, subject to the applicable rules. It is not a single age to assume for
  everyone. [Official age checker](https://www.gov.uk/state-pension-age).

### JSA, IS, ESA and UC

- **JSA — Jobseeker’s Allowance:** a benefit associated with looking for work.
  The frozen guidance distinguishes contribution-based JSA (`JSA(Cont)`) and
  income-based JSA (`JSA(IB)`). “New Style” JSA needs its own applicable guidance;
  do not treat the variants as interchangeable.
  [Official JSA guide](https://www.gov.uk/jobseekers-allowance).
- **IS — Income Support:** a benefit name retained in the historical guidance
  and evaluation questions. Its appearance is not evidence that a new claim is
  available. [Official Income Support page](https://www.gov.uk/income-support).
- **ESA — Employment and Support Allowance:** support associated with a health
  condition or disability affecting work. The frozen sources distinguish
  contributory ESA (`ESA(Cont)`) and income-related ESA (`ESA(IR)`). “New Style”
  ESA is a further regime distinction to check. [Official ESA guide](https://www.gov.uk/employment-support-allowance).
- **UC — Universal Credit:** a separate benefit named in modern guidance and
  transitions from older benefits. A DMG-only profile does not establish UC rules.
  [Official UC eligibility guide](https://www.gov.uk/universal-credit/eligibility).

“Contribution-based” concerns contribution conditions; “income-related” concerns
financial circumstances under the relevant rules. These short descriptions do
not replace the full conditions, exceptions or transition provisions.

### PIP, DLA, Child DLA and AA

- **PIP — Personal Independence Payment:** help with extra living costs linked
  to disability or a long-term health condition. It has its own rules.
  [Official PIP guide](https://www.gov.uk/pip).
- **DLA — Disability Living Allowance:** a benefit name in both adult and child
  contexts; identify which context and territory is meant.
  [Official adult DLA guide](https://www.gov.uk/dla-disability-living-allowance-benefit).
- **Child DLA:** the question set's shorthand for Disability Living Allowance for
  children. It is not a licence to apply adult DLA or PIP rules to a child.
  [Official child DLA guide](https://www.gov.uk/disability-living-allowance-children).
- **AA — Attendance Allowance:** a separate disability-related benefit described
  in its own guidance. Do not confuse it with a similarly named allowance within
  another scheme. [Official AA guide](https://www.gov.uk/attendance-allowance).

Territory matters: services and benefit names can differ within the UK. The
official guides provide routes to the relevant administration; this glossary
does not map all devolved rules.

### IIDB and CA

- **IIDB — Industrial Injuries Disablement Benefit:** a scheme relating to
  disablement from certain work-related accidents or diseases.
  [Official IIDB guide](https://www.gov.uk/industrial-injuries-disablement-benefit).
- **CA — Carer’s Allowance:** a benefit for carers with its own conditions and
  interactions. It is not the same thing as the benefit received by the person
  being cared for. [Official CA guide](https://www.gov.uk/carers-allowance).

### SDA and War Pensions wording

- **SDA:** an unresolved abbreviation in the supplied question set. The public
  evaluation preserves it and requests clarification. Do not silently expand it
  or substitute a similar-sounding benefit or addition.
- **War Pensions:** preserve the supplied wording and establish the exact scheme,
  payment and circumstance before modelling an interaction. The
  [official War Pension Scheme guide](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/war-pension-scheme-wps)
  is a starting reference; its existence does not resolve the question's intended
  meaning or supply every interaction rule.

### Entitlement, payment and disqualification

- **Claimant:** a person making or holding a claim. Demonstrations use generic
  scenarios, not personal claimant records.
- **Entitlement:** whether the relevant legal conditions for a benefit are met.
- **Payment or payability:** whether an amount is payable in the circumstances.
  This is a separate question from entitlement and from the amount of an award.
- **Disqualification:** exclusion under a specific rule. Read what the rule
  disqualifies someone *from*; do not silently replace “receiving” with “being
  entitled”. The [imprisonment acceptance case](../evaluation/context-assembly/imprisonment-case.json)
  checks that distinction against the captured guidance.
- **Change of circumstance:** a change that may require a decision to be reviewed.
  The relevant effect depends on the benefit, facts, date and applicable rules.
- **Exception:** a qualification to a general rule that needs its own evidence.

## Understanding the evidence

### Scope and applicability

**Scope** is the declared boundary: topic, benefit variant, territory, period and
source collection. **Applicability** asks whether a statement covers the task or
circumstances being considered. A paragraph can be accurately quoted yet outside
the question's scope.

### Provenance and authority

**Provenance** is the record of where something came from and how it was changed:
for example, an official PDF, an extracted page and a project-authored concept.
**Authority** records the standing of the source or assertion. An official source
does not make every downstream interpretation official.

- **Source-backed:** linked to supporting material. This does not by itself mean
  complete, correctly interpreted or specialist-approved.
- **Normalised** (`normalized` in machine fields): transformed into a consistent
  representation. Here that can include unreviewed machine extraction.
- **Inferred or model-derived:** proposed through reasoning or AI processing,
  rather than directly stated as that relationship by the source.
- **Unreviewed:** the stated specialist or human review has not occurred.
- **Locator:** a pointer to a specific page or paragraph. A PDF page number can
  differ from the page number printed within the document.

### Dates

**Publication date** describes when material was published. **Capture or
observation date** describes when the project obtained or checked it. **Legal
effective date** describes when a provision applies. A source update date is yet
another event. Do not substitute one date for another; an unknown date should
remain unknown.

### Snapshot, version and hash

A **snapshot** preserves material from a particular acquisition. A **version**
identifies a particular release or repository commit. A **hash**, such as SHA-256,
is a calculated fingerprint of bytes used to detect changes. Matching hashes
support a claim of identical content; they do not establish truth, currency,
rights or legal authority.

### Context package, profile and budget

A **context package** is the evidence and explanatory metadata assembled for one
question. A **profile** declares supported concepts, links and required evidence
for a defined task scope. A **budget** bounds the package, such as record count,
relationship count, traversal depth or bytes. **Truncation** means material was
omitted to respect a limit. A client can separately truncate what reaches its AI.

A **byte** is a unit of stored data. Package bytes include structure and
provenance as well as prose. They are not interchangeable with model tokens.

### Fail closed

Report insufficient or conflicting evidence when the required support cannot be
established. Do not silently fill gaps using model memory or unrelated sources.
This makes incompleteness visible; it does not itself answer the substantive
question.

## Understanding the technology

### OKF and OKF plus

**OKF — Open Knowledge Format:** a way to package inspectable knowledge for
people and software. **OKF+** here means OKF 0.2 with the project's additional
semantic and consumer contracts. It is not a separate claim of complete
production assurance. A **bundle** is the collection of records and supporting
files delivered together. See [discovery and format choices](discovery.md).

### YAML-LD, JSON-LD and RDF

- **YAML:** a text format commonly used for structured, human-edited data.
- **YAML-LD:** a YAML representation of linked data. This project's authoring
  profile uses it to attach stable identities and meanings to records. See the
  [YAML-LD specification](https://w3c.github.io/yaml-ld/) and the
  [project's pinned format decisions](discovery.md); use does not imply full
  processor conformance.
- **JSON:** a structured data format commonly exchanged by software.
- **JSON-LD:** linked data expressed in JSON. Its context maps terms to explicit
  identifiers. [W3C JSON-LD specification](https://www.w3.org/TR/json-ld/).
- **RDF — Resource Description Framework:** a model for statements connecting a
  subject, a relationship and an object. Different file formats can represent
  that model. [W3C RDF primer](https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf11-primer/).

### Concept, ontology and graph

A **concept** is a named idea. An **entity** is something individually identified,
such as a benefit, document or page. An **identifier** gives it a stable reference.
An **ontology** describes categories and relationships with defined meanings.
A **graph** represents entities as nodes and relationships as connections.

An **assertion** is a recorded statement about those entities. A **predicate**
names the relationship, such as “references”. A **traversal** follows connections
to other records. Evidence, status and scope belong alongside an assertion; the
existence of a line in a graph does not make it an authoritative conclusion.

An **index** organises records for lookup. Full-corpus Ask uses an index of words
to find candidate pages, alongside the smaller existing concept and relationship
index. A word match and a governed relationship serve different purposes: neither
alone establishes that all evidence needed for an answer has been found.

**Semantic** means concerned with meaning: identifying what a term or connection
actually denotes. A **schema** specifies the expected structure of data. A
**contract** records requirements that producers and consumers agree to follow.
**Metadata** describes an item, such as its source, date or format.

### API, MCP and WebMCP

- **API — application programming interface:** a defined way for software to
  request a capability or data.
- **Protocol:** agreed rules for exchanging messages. An **SDK**, or software
  development kit, supplies reusable code for implementing those rules.
- **MCP — Model Context Protocol:** a protocol for exposing tools and other
  capabilities to AI clients. A **tool** is a named operation with specified
  inputs and outputs. The Ask OKF remote tool is read-only.
  [MCP specification](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/latest).
- **WebMCP:** a browser interface through which a web page exposes tools to
  supporting agents. It is a developing Community Group specification, not a
  guarantee that every browser or AI product supports it.
  [WebMCP specification](https://webmachinelearning.github.io/webmcp/).
- **Client / host:** the application calling a tool or supplying its result to
  an AI. **Server:** the service accepting those calls. **Endpoint:** the service
  address used for the calls. Tool availability and complete delivery need actual
  tests in the intended client.

**Read-only** means the operation does not change the source records. It does not
mean every returned statement is correct. **Deterministic** means the same
specified inputs and software produce the same result; this supports replay but
does not prove that the evidence is sufficient.

### HTTP, GET, POST and 405

**HTTP** is the protocol used for web requests; **HTTPS** protects the connection
with encryption. **GET** usually requests a representation at an address, as when
opening a link. **POST** sends a request body for processing. **405 Method Not
Allowed** means that the chosen request method is not supported at that address.

Ask OKF's remote endpoint accepts MCP POST calls. A normal GET visit can return
405 even while tool calls work. This is documented in the
[tested connection guide](remote-mcp-demo.md#a-browser-shows-405); the
[MCP transport specification](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/basic/transports)
describes the remote POST transport.

### LLM, RAG and token

**LLM — large language model:** an AI model that processes and generates language.
It may reason over an evidence package, but its generated explanation is not the
same artefact as that package. **RAG — retrieval-augmented generation:** supplying
retrieved material to a model before it generates a response. Retrieval alone
does not establish authority or completeness; Ask OKF adds inspectable selection
and evidence requirements.
The [original RAG research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401) describes the
retrieval-and-generation approach.

A **token** is a unit used by a model's text-processing system. It can represent a
word, part of a word or another text fragment. Token counts depend on the model's
encoding. This staff-question evaluation measures bytes, not tokens or prices,
and does not establish equivalent answer quality or cost savings.
See the [OpenAI explanation of tokens](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/4936856-what-are-tokens-and-how-to-count-them).

### Evaluation, CI, branch and pull request

An **evaluation** checks defined expectations against retained results. A
**rubric** states what those results must demonstrate. A **replay** reruns checks
against saved inputs and responses; a **receipt** records what was observed and
which versions were used.

**CI — continuous integration:** automated checks run as changes are integrated.
A **repository** stores the project's files and history. A **branch** holds a
line of changes; a **pull request (PR)** presents proposed changes for review
before they are merged. **Branch protection** requires selected conditions before
updates to a branch. See [this repository's actual settings](repository-governance.md).
