# The ADM manual is now acquired

**Advice for decision making (ADM)** is a DWP staff guide distinct from the
Decision makers’ guide (DMG). Both can be relevant to a benefits question, but
the applicable benefit variant, date and circumstances must be established.
The [official ADM publication](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/advice-for-decision-making-staff-guide)
describes its scope; [DWP explains the guide distinction](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/decision-makers-guide-vol-1-decision-making-and-appeals-staff-guide).

The project has acquired **all 182 PDFs and 4,347 measured pages** listed in the
ADM publication observed on 19 September 2026. The earlier 15 September discovery
contained metadata only; this acquisition adds original PDFs, page text,
hashes, exact page links and extraction-quality flags. See the
[source snapshot and verification instructions](../source/adm-2026-09-19/README.md).

The separately preserved DMG snapshot contains 331 PDFs and 14,743 measured
pages. Together the two source families comprise **513 PDF records and 19,090
measured pages**. These are acquisition counts, including listed supplementary,
spare and historical material; they are not counts of reviewed rules or answered
questions.

The ADM capture has **91 pages with no machine-extracted text**. Those pages
remain in the inventory and retain their original PDF links. No OCR or exhaustive
visual review has established whether they are blank or image-only.

Source acquisition, full-corpus discovery, governed evidence assembly and an AI's
answer are separate stages. The preserved 52-record custody profile and its
earlier acceptance receipts remain version-specific. Adding this manual does
not retrospectively make that profile cover ADM or answer its unsupported tasks.
The [learning path](learning-path.md) explains how to inspect scope, authority,
provenance and gaps before using a result.

This is an independent experimental publication, not an official DWP product or
individual benefits advice. Machine extraction and authored classifications
remain unreviewed; specialist acceptance has not been established.
