# Care-home component qualification review

21 September 2026. **Model-authored source review; not specialist acceptance, current legal advice or an entitlement decision.** The original investigation was read-only. The implementation section records the subsequently authorised housing and temporary-care changes. The larger disability-addition options remain proposals. No frozen source, trial or review obligation changed.

## Result and proposed order

1. **Housing costs:** correct two scope phrases before declaring dependencies. The source concerns housing-cost treatment during an absence, not permission to stay in a care home. It excludes Housing Benefit expenditure defined as an element for which Housing Benefit **may be payable**, rather than only expenditure actually met by Housing Benefit. Five already-modelled whole pages support a bounded summary.
2. **Temporary care-home residence:** restore the explicit **claimant with no partner** qualification to the opening statement. Two already-modelled pages carry the operative paragraphs and complete example.
3. **No-partner disability addition:** avoid equating every occurrence of “in receipt” with actual current payment. The captured chapter has deemed-receipt provisions on pages 16–17, which are acquired but not in the semantic base. An eight-page support group covers the current named material with its example continuation; a ten-page group is justified if the definition explicitly incorporates the receipt qualifications. The Scottish amendment dependencies remain separate and version-qualified.
4. **Severe-disability addition overview:** prefer a short navigation/identity definition, with four supporting pages. Keeping its statement about Scottish amendments requires the actual memo support as well. Do not make every generic overview activate every detailed condition branch.

These are distinct from full answer closure. They do not resolve funding-specific disability-benefit cessation, legal versions, judgments, household facts or the other recorded obligations.

## Exact replacements for the two immediate scope defects

These replacements were approved for the bounded implementation described below. They remain model-authored interpretations requiring specialist review.

**Housing-cost definition, replace the final exclusion sentence:**

> Permanent residence and Housing Benefit expenditure are separate exclusions. DMG 78271 defines Housing Benefit expenditure as an element for which Housing Benefit may be payable; actual payment of Housing Benefit is not stated as a condition here.

Also replace its sentence beginning “DMG 78240 permits a trial stay” with:

> DMG 78240 allows treatment as living in the former home, with housing costs allowed, for a trial period of up to 13 weeks, subject to assessment of suitability, intention to return if unsuitable and the normally occupied part of the home not being let or sublet. This temporary-absence treatment is subject to the stated overall maximum of 52 weeks.

**Temporary-care-home definition, replace the opening sentence:**

> For a claimant with no partner whose normal home circumstances do not satisfy the additional-amount conditions, DMG 78084 says a temporary care-home stay does not itself change their normal residence.

This narrower sentence leaves the following 78085 partner branch and the 78086 DLA-payability conjunction intact. It does not assert that living with any other person automatically prevents the addition.

### Reviewed literal before this increment: `care-home-housing-costs`

> DMG 78270 concerns the housing-cost additional amount, not the whole Pension Credit award. It states that an additional amount for housing costs is not allowed for claimants in a care home unless their absence from the dwelling normally occupied as home is temporary. The exception does not establish payment of care-home fees. DMG 78240 permits a trial stay of up to 13 weeks only with suitability assessment, intention to return if unsuitable and an unlet home; the overall absence limit is 52 weeks. Permanent residence and expenditure met by Housing Benefit are separate exclusions. The complete paragraphs span adjacent pages.

## B. Temporary-care-home component

**Require pages 78/24 and 78/25 (two pages), both already modelled.** Page 24 supplies **78084–78086**; page 25 completes the Malcolm/Wendy example, including the note that the lower-rate addition is not appropriate in that respite-care example. The example must not become a general four-week cessation rule.

- **78084 is under “Claimants who have no partner”.** Its negative example concerns a person whose normal home circumstances already do not meet the additional-amount conditions. A temporary stay does not by itself change where the claimant normally resides.
- **78085** is the separate partner branch: temporary separation preserves household membership and normal residence, with the paragraph's statement about the amount payable.
- **78086** requires review where residential care exceeds 28 days **and DLA payability ceases**. Keep both conditions and the distinction between review, component withdrawal and the whole award.
- Footnotes include SPC Regulations Schedule I paragraph 1(1) and regulation 5(2). These statutory bodies are acquired, but funding-specific DLA payment rules and other benefit variants are not established by these pages.

**Narrow the first sentence:** “For a claimant with no partner whose normal home circumstances fail the additional-amount conditions, DMG 78084 says temporary admission does not itself change their normal residence.” This preserves the heading and avoids suggesting that every person who lives with somebody is disqualified; ignored-resident rules may be relevant.

**Optional further evidence:** page 78/17 (**78065–78066**) defines normal residence and includes respite-care examples. It is acquired but not in the semantic base. Use it if actually explaining normal residence. The ignored-person group, pages 78/21–78/23, is needed if the answer decides which other residents count. Do not silently infer those facts. Chapter 77 household qualifications are a separate declared dependency if explaining household status beyond the limited temporary branch.

**Activation:** the permanent-residence wording in Staff 012 and 013 does not justify making every temporary rule an unconditional profile requirement. If the overview retains a permanent/temporary contrast, the selected temporary summary must carry these two pages. A specific temporary/respite-care question should have its own explicit profile. Staff 018's stated permanent “all scenarios” question still needs the boundary explained, without converting it into an unrestricted temporary-care answer.
